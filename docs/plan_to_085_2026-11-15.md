## 2. The architecture we are building

```
Photo ─┬─► READER A  Qwen3.8-27B FINE-TUNED on the teachers' labels          ─┐
       │            (writes the teacher-style answer + classification layer; │
       │             2-3 training runs averaged)                              │
       │                                                                      ├─► JUDGE (trained) ─► blend, cross-fitted weights ─► balance ─► top 3
       ├─► READER B  Qwen3.8-27B untrained, MAXIMUM thinking, every photo    ─┤        ▲
       │                                                                      │        │
       ├─► READER C  a different model, untrained (second opinion; optional) ─┘        │
       │                                                                               │
       └─► old system (kept only while the development set shows it still adds) ───────┘
```

What changes: the model that knows the teachers' habits becomes Qwen3.8-27B (fixes W1, W2); every photo gets a full-effort
reading (W3); a trained judge makes the final call (W4) from Qwen3.8's transcription (W5); the key parts are trained more
than once (W6).

### E1. Fine-tune Qwen3.8-27B, with the classification layer (ready, not launched)

| | |
|---|---|
| What | Train the 27B model on the 1,199 teacher-labelled photos to write the teacher's transcription, error line and note, then the code. A new layer on top scores all 76 codes directly |
| Fixes | W1, W2 |

**If it fails:**
- *The smoke test crashes (memory or library error):* it runs on 12 examples before anything else, so the loss is under $2. Fix on the pod; if 80 GB is not enough, drop the transcription from the training answer (halves the text) or train fewer layers.
- *No A100 80 GB in stock:* run on the 48 GB card with the model compressed to 4-bit. Only the classification layer can then be used for scoring, not the written answers. Needs a small code change.
- *Training is slower than planned:* the schedule is tied to the clock, so the run still ends on time with a complete, shorter schedule.
- *Written answers get worse than the untrained model (the known risk of training a thinking model):* keep the classification layer, discard the written answers, and keep untrained Qwen3.8 as the reasoning reader.
- *Strong on seen errors, weak on hard rows:* that is still useful. Use it where the question has teacher labels, and let untrained Qwen3.8 and the judge lead on new errors.
- *No gain anywhere:* stop. Loss about $9. Fall back to E3 and E5 (untrained levers) plus the judge; realistic ceiling then about 0.79-0.80.

## 9. Research update, 15 Nov 03:37 UTC: what our own data says, and what changes

Stage A finished tonight, so Qwen3.8's untrained answer now exists for all 1,500 teacher-labelled photos. These checks cost nothing.

### Findings

| # | Finding | Numbers | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Qwen3.8's naming mistakes are not systematic | 478 wrong first codes of 1,500. 279 different (teacher code, Qwen code) confusions; 187 happen once; only 40% of errors sit in confusions seen three or more times. Replacing Qwen's code by the teachers' most common code for it gives 0.679 top-1 against 0.681 | There is no translation table to learn. Each case needs understanding, which is why small models and prompt tricks failed and a large trained model is required |
| F2 | A few confusions are large enough to fix by rule | Teacher EXP-04 called EXP-07 twenty times. Qwen uses WOP-01 105 times against the teachers' 57, and CAL-01 95 against 57 | A short "how the teachers draw this boundary" note in the prompt for the top ten confusions could remove up to about a tenth of the errors. Free to include in E3 |
| F3 | Students on the same question mostly make different mistakes | Median 3 labelled students per question. Two students on one question share a code 26-44% of the time; only 2 pairs of workings are near-identical | Memorising per question is nearly used up (the old system already has 0.87 on seen errors). Per-photo understanding is where the remaining points are |
| F4 | Sharing predictions between similar workings on a question does not help | Best setting +0.0003 on 1,500 rows | Rejected |
| F5 | Matching a student's final answer to a labelled student's final answer does not help | Covers 19 of 200 dev error rows, right 58% of the time | Rejected |
| F6 | A judge made of simple features on this Mac does not help | Alone 0.751 against 0.748 for Qwen3.8; fused -0.010 | Rejected. Confirms the judging step needs a large model |
| F7 | The development set is representative | Qwen3.8 alone: 0.744 on the 1,199 other training photos, 0.759 on development | Pass marks on development can be trusted to about +/-0.02 |
| F8 | The server was overloaded in every earlier Qwen3.8 run | 40 parallel requests: about 170 tokens/s. 20-30: 400-530 | Every Qwen3.8 reading costs about 2.5 times less than budgeted on 14 Nov |

### Is there a maximum score?

**Not sure. I have no evidence of a ceiling, and none that 0.85 is out of reach.**

- I tried to measure how consistent the teachers' labels are, by comparing near-identical workings on the same question. There are only 2 such pairs in 1,500 photos, so label noise cannot be measured this way.
- What is proven reachable is 0.8465, the current best on the leaderboard. Six teams sit within 0.005 of it, which is consistent with a plateau for the methods in use, but it does not prove a limit.
- The route in this plan is estimated to reach 0.82-0.84. Getting to 0.85 would need the hard rows to improve more than any step here is known to deliver; I cannot say whether that will happen, only that nothing rules it out.

### On a maximum score

| Source | What it says | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Host, competition data page | "some codes have close neighbours on which even trained annotators sometimes differ. Expect some label noise in both train and test". | ScratchMath paper (closest published task) | Human experts 78-82% correct on 7 error categories from handwritten work; best models 44-49% | Different task and far fewer categories; shows experts themselves are not near 100% on this kind of judgement |
| MAP 2025 competition | Annotator agreement kappa 0.70-0.90; winner 0.949 MAP@3 | With cleaner labels and 2-4 codes per question, models got very high |

**Conclusion: still "not sure".** There is label noise, so some limit exists below 1.0, but nothing published places it near 0.85.

### On how to fine-tune (checked against published recipes)

| Point | Source | Our code |
|---|---|---|
| LoRA rank 8-16, learning rate 1e-4 to 2e-4, full-precision (bf16) weights, all linear layers, 1-2 epochs | Unsloth, ModelScope ms-swift and NVIDIA recipes for Qwen3.5/3.8 | Matches (rank 16, 1e-4, bf16, all linear layers, 2 epochs) |
| For training without thinking, put the empty think block in the prompt and take no loss on it | ms-swift guide | Matches |
| Install the linear-attention kernels (flash-linear-attention, causal-conv1d) or training is slow | ms-swift guide | In the pod script |
| Merge the trained layers into the model before serving; loading them at run time in vLLM is unreliable for this architecture | vLLM pull requests closed unmerged | Matches: we merge |
| The winner of MAP 2025 read the last token's state into a linear layer over the question's candidate codes; the written-rationale loss added little | MAP 1st-place write-up | Supports the classification layer as the main output. We keep the written answer as a second output and decide on development |
| Single training runs are unstable; averaging seeds beat averaging folds; winners retrained on all data with three seeds | MAP 1st-place write-up | Already E2; now confirmed as standard practice |
| Middle layers often separate classes better than the last layer | Paper (abstract only) | E1a saves five layers and picks by cross-validation |
| 4-bit training is discouraged for this model family | Unsloth Qwen3.5 guide (its Qwen3.8 page disagrees) | We use full precision on an 80 GB card; the 4-bit fallback in E1 is now a last resort |

- **Winners' training and inference pipelines will be checked by the host.** Our scripts, logs and `HANDOFF.md` are the record. Claude-labelled unlabelled photos are allowed; final scoring must be offline, which it is.

### New lever found tonight: the final answer

On the 1,500 teacher-labelled photos, a correct final answer means "no major error" 94% of the time (487 of 517), and a wrong
final answer means an error 98% of the time (964 of 983). Untrained Qwen3.8 breaks this rule on 70 photos: it says "no error"
on 45 photos whose final answer is wrong, and flags an error on 25 whose final answer is right.

- If we knew for every photo whether the final answer is right, the development score would rise by up to **+0.020**
  (measured with the teachers' own flag, which we do not have for test photos, so this is an upper bound).
- A crude text match between Qwen3.8's transcription and the answer key agrees with the teachers only 79% of the time and
  gives no gain. The signal has to be learned.
- So the fine-tune now trains one extra yes/no output on the classification layer: "is the final answer correct?", using the
  teachers' flag on all training photos. Coded and dry-run; costs nothing extra to train.

### A possible path to 0.90 (arithmetic, not a forecast)

| Loss | Now | Recovered if the fix works well | Left |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naming | 34.0 | 60% (20.4) | 13.6 |

| Error or no error | 16.0 | 40% (6.4) | 9.6 |
| Total | 61.3 | 31.3 | 30.0, which is 0.90 |

Recovering 60% of the naming loss is the part nobody has shown yet; it is what the judge and the fine-tune are for, and their
development results are the test of whether this path is real. Label disagreement between teachers (section 10) may put part of
